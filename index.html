Tomorrow in Shinjuku

A plan for the next day or two: police report, money, wallet. The police station and the MoneyGram counter are both in Nishi-Shinjuku, about a 10-minute walk apart, so you can do both in one morning.

Go to a hospital now if any of these happen

  • Headache getting worse, vomiting, or feeling very sleepy or confused
  • Vision in the hurt eye gets blurry, doubled, or darker
  • New swelling or bleeding

Ambulance: 119 (free, say "English"). For an English-speaking doctor: Himawari 03-5285-8181, every day 9:00–20:00.

Step 1

Shinjuku Police Station

Shinjuku Police Station 新宿警察署　東京都新宿区西新宿6-1-1 03-3346-0110 · Open in Google Maps

Go to the station itself, not a small police box (kōban). Only the station writes a formal report. If the club was outside Shinjuku (Roppongi, Shibuya), they may send you to that area's station.

Bring:

  • Passport
  • Hospital papers and receipt (ask the hospital for a shindansho, a medical certificate, if you don't have one)
  • Photos of your injuries
  • Club name, address, date and time, anything you remember about the staff
  • Screenshots of any strange card charges

Ask for two things: a damage report for the assault (被害届) and a lost property report for the wallet (遺失届). Write down the report number. Your insurance and your bank will ask for it.

Tell them you think you were drugged, right at the start. Some drugs can still be found in a test for a few days, so earlier is better.

Before you go, fill in the Incident Statement. You tick what happened in English and it writes a full statement in Japanese for the officer, so they hear the whole story and not "too much to drink".

Show this to the officer

アメリカから来た旅行者です。日本語は少し話せますが、手続きは英語の通訳をお願いします。

（日時）、（場所）で、店の警備員に暴行されました。けがをして病院に行きました。

飲み物に薬を入れられた可能性があります。財布もなくなりました。

被害届と遺失届を出したいです。受理番号をください（保険のため）。

It says: I'm a tourist from the US and need an English interpreter. I was assaulted by the venue's security staff and went to hospital. I may have been drugged. My wallet is gone. I want to file a damage report and a lost property report, and I need the report numbers for insurance.

Step 2

Cards and money

  • Lock every card first in your bank app, or call the number on your bank's website. Ask them to dispute any charges you didn't make, and give them the police report number.
  • Visa and Mastercard can arrange emergency cash or a replacement card (often 1–3 business days) once your bank approves. Ask your bank about it when you call. Visa's worldwide collect line: +1-303-967-1096.
  • If your card is still in Apple Pay or Google Pay and the bank didn't cancel it, most convenience stores and train gates in Tokyo accept phone payment.

Picking up a MoneyGram transfer

enRemit (MoneyGram partner) 東京都新宿区西新宿6-12-1 パークウエストビル 13階 03-3359-0009 · Mon–Fri 9:00–19:00 · Open in Google Maps
  • Without registering you can receive up to ¥100,000 (or about $900) per transfer.
  • Bring your passport and the 8-digit reference number from the person sending the money.
  • Call before you go to confirm they're paying out MoneyGram today. Closed on weekends.

Source: enRemit. Other locations: MoneyGram's map.

Backup: the US Embassy. American Citizen Services can help your family send you money through the State Department, and can replace a passport if it was in the wallet. 03-3224-5000 (24 hours for emergencies). How it works.

Step 3

The wallet

  • Wallets turned in anywhere in Tokyo go into the police system. The lost property report from Step 1 is what links it back to you.
  • You can also search online: Tokyo Police lost and found.
  • If you took a train or taxi that night, those companies keep their own lost and found for a few days before sending items to the police.
